# Two-stage patient-adaptive acquisition: 48 initial plus 16 follow-up wells

## Question and workflow boundary
All recent models used an input plan committed before the patient's responses were observed. This trial asks whether the SAME total 64 treatment measurements are more informative when 48 are obtained first and the remaining 16 are chosen from those readings. The numerical aspiration remains MSE <=0.000521372861048106 and p90 <=0.037419695944064885 for the original 24 AUCs. It is not achieved by starting this trial.

This is a NEW sequential workflow, not a drop-in replacement for the existing single-round plate plan. It requires an additional measurement/decision round and potentially different follow-up doses for different organoids. Historical same-plate replay cannot establish that delayed assays behave identically. No claim of identical elapsed laboratory time, material cost, asynchronous assay comparability or prospective feasibility is made. Physical treatment count remains 64, with 32 from each source plate, no repeated native dose and no excluded query concentration. All 24 targets remain present with eight two-dose and sixteen three-dose outputs.

The two alternative initial layouts A and B are separately evaluated. Their first two doses use complementary plates, but their final 16 choices can differ, so final layouts are not asserted to be complements. The old fixed-layout validator is NOT changed or used to certify this new sequential protocol. Any gain needs separate scientific review and a staged-assay plan before promotion. The user-authorized 64-well accuracy goal does not silently authorize hiding these workflow changes.

## Prior and predictors, fixed before outcomes
Reuse the numeric smooth-shape-mixture construction from empirical_shape_mixture64/model.py at commit b4b1d54, copied byte-for-byte as shape_prior.py. That model previously failed as a fixed-plan predictor. This is NOT a rerun with a new name: the experiment now tests whether its conditional error model can select follow-up measurements. Its sigmoid dictionary, patient weighting, residual covariance, scale and shrinkage are unchanged and not retuned.

Fit all priors only on outer-training patients' full historical curves. For each target, the initial two doses are the fitting-only R13 best-two subset. Initial physical layout is [p1,p2] for A and [p2,p1] for B. For each eligible remaining native dose and each plate, prepare a three-input ridge head, at penalty0.01 and scale floor0.05, using the initial pair and that third physical cell. Fit using training rows and their complementary layouts with equal total weight per patient. The two-input head is fit the same way. No evaluation rows or labels enter any fit.

PRIMARY estimator is this own-drug ridge prediction. SECONDARY estimator is the unchanged mixture posterior AUC mean from exactly the same paid values. Neither includes a cross-drug residual kernel, a post-outcome fallback, fitted ensemble, or target-wise choice between estimators. The secondary is not a model selected to rescue the primary.

## Selection objective
After observing a patient's initial two values for a target, calculate the expected squared error of each fixed ridge head under the fitting curve mixture. The candidate third value is integrated out analytically, NOT read. Conditional Gaussian means/covariance plus posterior mixture masses give the exact model-based loss of the linear ridge estimator. This is a surrogate for true biological error, not a calibrated uncertainty or guaranteed improvement.

For each target the choices are no third dose, its lowest-risk remaining dose on p1, or its lowest-risk remaining dose on p2. Dynamic programming selects exactly eight follow-up wells on each plate, at most one per target. Thus exactly sixteen targets get a third dose, and every policy uses 48+16=64 distinct native-dose observations. Dose and plate ties resolve by fixed numeric option order. All sixteen requests are committed BEFORE any follow-up value is revealed; there is no third round or hidden lookahead.

ADAPTIVE policy uses the above conditional risks. MATCHED STATIC policy uses the same heads and mixture but minimizes their unconditional expected squared error before any initial values are read. It has the same irrevocable initial-pair constraint, stage budgets, candidate catalog and final estimators. Its follow-up requests are identical across query patients. This control distinguishes response adaptation from changing the dose menu or estimator.

Four fixed results are reported: static+ridge, adaptive+ridge (PRIMARY), static+mixture, adaptive+mixture. Each is a separate hypothetical deployment. Predictions from different layouts or acquisition policies are never averaged or assembled target by target. Evaluating competing policies on historical data does not mean their union of measurements is available to any one policy.

## Data and evaluation
Authenticated Lib1 TRAIN: 119 organoid samples, 59 whole patients, all 24 unchanged raw normalized log-dose AUCs. Preserve the original eligible query catalog. Full historical response values can fit training distributions and verify endpoint algebra, but evaluated patients' full curves are hidden behind a request-logging oracle. The policy API accepts only the 48 first-round values; the estimator API accepts those and the sixteen selected follow-up values. No Protected22/Lib2, new controls, genotype, patient-ID feature, external biological response, network or model API is used.

Five original whole-patient outer folds, no outcome-selected model/parameter menu and therefore no inner hyperparameter selection. Every original best-two planner, curve mixture and ridge head is rebuilt on that fold's training patients. Saved operating64 and retained64 predictions are comparators only. Never recycle global outer OOF arrays as fitting data. Equal-patient/equal-target mean of A/B squared LOSSES, p90 patient RMSE, patient/fold/target breadth. All four results finish before overall losses are opened.

Eligibility for a separate fresh end-to-end replay requires lower mean, >=30 patient wins, all five favorable folds and nonworse p90 against both retained64 and operating64. Half-error has the absolute threshold above, plus nonworse p90. There is no automatic promotion even after those numeric gates, because the acquisition workflow changed. Preserve R13/R18 comparisons before any future review. No Kaggle writeup/runtime/demo change is part of this trial.

Bootstrap100000 whole-patient draws, seed202610080930, descriptive and selection-unadjusted. The already inspected Lib1 population remains adaptive development, not independent biological validation. Finish and preserve negative outcomes; no post-outcome menu revisions, dose-budget changes or silent retries.

## Tests and replay
Synthetic tests independently check the conditional risk formula, zero-error known functional, unconditional risk, exact DP versus exhaustive enumeration, deterministic ties, patient-weighted ridge symmetry, staged API isolation, physical budgets, missing-input rejection and numeric state replay. Perturb every unpurchased query value and verify the committed requests/predictions do not change. Change outerfold0's training-ineligible labels/full curves but preserve its actual paid query values; rebuilding all fitted states and policies must be invariant. Save each fold's numeric bank and per-patient request traces privately. Independently recompute posterior risk, the constrained allocation, ridge and mixture predictions, and all metrics from those banks without reusing the policy implementation. Passing these checks is numerical integrity, not fresh biological validation.

## Established method references
Cohn, Ghahramani and Jordan, Active Learning with Statistical Models (1996), https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9603104 . Statistical experimental design and Gaussian-mixture active selection are established methods; no invention claim.
Bornkamp et al., Response-adaptive dose-finding under model uncertainty (2011), https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5883 . Its study motivates the general response-adaptive principle, not the safety, efficiency or accuracy of this organoid workflow. No clinical dose-assignment recommendation is made here.
